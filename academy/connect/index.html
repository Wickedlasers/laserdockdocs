How this course works

This course is about what keeps your cube connected and up to date: the four connection modes, what the cube’s screen tells you, the web admin page, firmware updates and a factory reset. It ends with power, heat, and when to stop and contact us.

By the end you can

  • choose the right connection for one cube, several cubes, or a place with busy WiFi;
  • connect over the cube’s own WiFi, your router or a network cable, including a cable straight to a laptop;
  • read the status bar and the Status Info page, and find out why a projection breaks up;
  • use the web admin page, update the firmware and do a factory reset;
  • charge the cube correctly, keep it within its temperature range, and know when to stop using it and contact support.

  • Pass, then download your certificate as a PDF or an image.

Lesson 1 of 8

Choose a connection

The cube needs a network connection when something streams to it or controls it over the network: LaserOS and other show software, an Art‑Net desk, or the web apps in your browser. In Playlist, Visualizer and Laser Show mode it plays from its SD card, and a DMX desk has its own cable.

Beginners guide, part 4: connection modesWatch on YouTube

Filmed on firmware 2.0 or older. On firmware 2.1 and later, changing the connection mode doesn’t restart the cube.

The four connection modes

  • WiFi server: the cube makes its own WiFi network, and your phone or computer joins it. The manual suggests it for one cube without cables, where WiFi isn’t congested (lesson 2).
  • WiFi client: the cube joins your WiFi router. How well it works depends on the router, how congested the WiFi is and how much other traffic is on the network (lesson 2).
  • LAN client: the cube joins your network through a network cable, to a router or a switch. The manual recommends it for several cubes, and where WiFi may be congested (lesson 3).
  • LAN server: a cable straight to one device, such as a laptop, or a phone with the USB‑to‑LAN adapter that comes with the cube. The cube gives that device its network address (lesson 3).

Where to set it

  • Settings → Connection chooses the mode. Firmware 2.1 and later switch straight away. Older firmware asks to restart: move the red cursor to Yes and push the knob. The boot screen shows the firmware version when the cube starts (firmware 2.0 and later).
  • Settings → Network holds each mode’s own settings, such as network names, passwords and addresses. Cube Link is there too; it’s only available when the connection mode isn’t WiFi.
  • Before you use any wireless mode, connect the WiFi antenna that comes with the cube.
  • The status bar shows the connection mode, with a tick once it’s connected (lesson 4).

Check yourself

Question 1At a party, the cube plays a playlist from its SD card, with nothing streaming to it. Does it need a network connection?

Playlist, Visualizer and Laser Show mode play from the SD card. You only need a connection to stream to the cube or control it over the network.

Question 2You’ll stream from LaserOS to six cubes in a hall full of WiFi networks. Which connection does the manual recommend?

For several cubes, and where WiFi may be congested, the manual recommends network cables in LAN client mode.

Question 3Your cube runs firmware 2.1. You change Settings → Connection from WiFi server to LAN client. What happens next?

Firmware 2.1 and later change the connection mode without a restart. Older firmware asks to restart: select Yes and push the knob.

Lesson 2 of 8

WiFi: the cube’s network or yours

Over WiFi, the cube either makes its own network (WiFi server) or joins yours (WiFi client). Both use the 2.4GHz band, which many other devices share.

WiFi server: the cube’s own network

  • Connect the WiFi antenna, then choose Settings → Connection → WiFi Server.
  • On your phone or computer, join the cube’s network. By default it’s called LaserCube and the password is Laser2020.
  • Its settings are under Settings → Network → WiFi Server: network name (SSID), password, channel, bandwidth, IP address and net mask.
  • Leave the channel on Auto: each time the cube is switched on, it then looks for the quietest WiFi channel.
  • The WiFi Scan button on that page shows how busy each 2.4GHz channel is, and picks the quietest one when the scan ends. It only works in WiFi server mode; in other modes it’s greyed out.
  • A list that’s mostly red means too many other 2.4GHz devices are around, as in some apartment buildings. Then consider a cable instead, with the USB‑to‑LAN adapter (lesson 3). Outdoors, WiFi should be fine.

WiFi client: joining your router

  • Under Settings → Network → WiFi Client, enter your router’s network name (SSID) and password. Then choose WiFi Client under Settings → Connection. Your phone or computer must be on the same router’s network.
  • IP Mode Auto takes an address from the router. Static lets you type the IP address, the gateway (your router’s address) and the net mask (also called the subnet mask) yourself.
  • To type a name or an address, highlight the box and short‑press the knob. Select the tick to close the box.

When WiFi quality matters

  • Mostly in LaserOS mode: it streams the laser data over the network at a high rate, so a congested network shows as break‑ups in the projection.
  • Playlist and Visualizer mode draw their content from the SD card, so WiFi quality shouldn’t affect their projection.

Check yourself

Question 1You join the cube’s own WiFi for the first time. What are the factory network name and password?

By default the cube’s network is called LaserCube, with the password Laser2020. You can change both under Settings → Network → WiFi Server.

Question 2The WiFi Scan button is greyed out. Why?

WiFi Scan only works in WiFi server mode. In the other connection modes it’s greyed out.

Question 3The scan list is mostly red, and the LaserOS projection keeps breaking up. What does the manual suggest?

Mostly red means the 2.4GHz channels are congested. A wired connection, with the USB‑to‑LAN adapter, avoids the lost data.

In the manual: WiFi server mode·WiFi client mode·LaserOS control mode

Lesson 3 of 8

Network cables and direct connections

A network cable avoids WiFi congestion. The cube’s network port takes a standard network (Ethernet) cable. A phone, tablet or laptop without a network port can use the USB‑to‑LAN adapter that comes with the cube.

LAN client: through a router or a switch

  • The cube joins your network through the cable. The manual recommends this for several cubes, and where WiFi may be congested.
  • Connected to your router, or to a switch that’s connected to it, the cube gets its address from the router, for example one starting 192.168.
  • Under Settings → Network → LAN Client, IP Mode Auto IP takes an address from the router, or lets the cube give itself one when there’s no router (below). Static IP lets you type the IP address, net mask and gateway (the gateway is your router’s address).

A cable straight to one computer: two ways

  • LAN client, with a self‑assigned address: with no router on the cable, nothing hands out addresses. After about 20 seconds the cube gives itself one starting 169.254 (an APIPA address; the manual’s diagram says to allow up to a minute). The status bar then shows A‑IP before the address, and a tick next to LAN‑C.
  • Leave the computer’s network adapter on automatic (DHCP), not a fixed address: it gives itself a 169.254 address too, and the two then find each other. There’s no internet over this cable; that’s expected.
  • LAN server: the cube gives the connected device its address. The manual recommends it for a direct cable to one device, such as a laptop, or a phone with the USB‑to‑LAN adapter. Its IP address and net mask are under Settings → Network → LAN Server.

Both ways work for one computer on a cable.

Steps: LaserOS over a direct cable, in LAN client mode

For LAN client, the manual gives these steps:

  1. Turn off the computer’s WiFi for now, and close LaserOS if it’s running.
  2. Switch the cube on and connect the cable between the cube and the computer.
  3. Wait until the cube shows its 169.254 address.
  4. Start LaserOS and check that its Laser ON button isn’t greyed out: then LaserOS has found the cube.
  5. Turn the computer’s WiFi back on if you need the internet.

Check yourself

Put the manual’s steps for LaserOS over a direct cable (LAN client) in order.

  1. Turn off the computer’s WiFi and close LaserOS
  2. Switch the cube on and connect the cable to the computer
  3. Wait until the cube shows its 169.254 address
  4. Start LaserOS and check that Laser ON isn’t greyed out
  5. Turn the computer’s WiFi back on if you need the internet

LaserOS starts last, once the cube has its self‑assigned address. If the Laser ON button isn’t greyed out, LaserOS has found the cube.

Question 1A cable runs straight from the cube, in LAN client mode, to your laptop. What address does the cube show after about 20 seconds?

With no router to hand out addresses, the cube gives itself an APIPA address starting 169.254, shown with A‑IP in the status bar.

Question 2In that setup, how should the laptop’s network adapter be set?

On automatic, the laptop also gives itself a 169.254 address, and the two find each other. The manual’s diagram says never to use a manual IP here.

Question 3In which mode does the cube give your laptop its address over a direct cable?

In LAN server mode the cube hands out the address to the one connected device.

Lesson 4 of 8

What the screen tells you

The status bar at the bottom of the cube’s screen gives you a quick overview of the cube, and shows important messages and warnings.

Beginners guide, part 3: CubeOS overviewWatch on YouTube

The status bar

  • Battery: the battery gauge. With Battery Pct turned on (Settings → General), it also shows the percentage from time to time.
  • I‑LOCK: a warning that the safety interlock is open (the manual’s picture says “disabled”), so the laser won’t emit (Start here, lesson 6).
  • The control mode, such as LaserOS or Playlist.
  • The connection mode, such as LAN‑C (LAN client), WiFi‑C (WiFi client) or WiFi‑S (WiFi server). A tick next to it means it’s connected; a red cross means it isn’t.
  • The cube’s IP address, at the bottom right. A‑IP before it means a self‑assigned address (lesson 3). In Playlist and Visualizer mode, your browser opens the web app at this address (Control modes, lesson 3 (Playlist) and lesson 4 (Visualizer)). In LaserOS mode it opens the web admin page (lesson 7).
  • The background colour: green while laser data arrives over the network, cyan while the ILDA input is in use (it overrides the current control mode), and purple on a Cube Link slave that has found its master.

Preview

Preview, in the main menu, shows a live picture of what the cube draws. It works for data arriving over the network and for the cube’s own content, not for the ILDA input. Settings → General sets it to 2D or 3D.

General and language settings

  • Settings → General: the screen brightness, Battery Pct, the preview style (2D or 3D), and backward compatibility.
  • Backward compatibility is only needed if you use older WiFi cubes and Ultra MK2 or Pro cubes on the same network with LaserOS. You also need LaserOS 0.17.7 or later.
  • Settings → Language: 12 menu languages (English by default). Choosing another one restarts the cube. Some labels are shortened to fit the screen.

Check yourself

Question 1The status bar shows a red cross next to LAN‑C. What does it mean?

A tick next to the connection mode means connected; a red cross means not connected.

Question 2The status bar turns cyan. What’s happening?

Cyan means the ILDA input is in use, overriding the current control mode. Green is network data; purple is a Cube Link slave that found its master.

Question 3The status bar shows A‑IP before the cube’s address. What does it mean?

A‑IP marks a self‑assigned (APIPA) address starting 169.254, for example on a direct cable in LAN client mode.

Lesson 5 of 8

The Status Info page

Status Info, in the main menu, shows what the cube’s hardware and network are doing: the battery, the data buffer, the SD card, and counters for data received and data lost.

What each line means

  • Battery: an estimate of the charge, worked out from the battery’s voltage. While the cube projects, it moves around and reads a little lower as the image and output power change. While the cube charges, the charge can’t be measured.
  • Remote Slaves: with Cube Link, how many slave cubes are connected.
  • Buffer Free and Buffer Size: the free space in the cube’s data buffer, and the most it can hold.
  • SD Card Capacity and SD Card Free: the card’s size and its free space, in gigabytes.
  • PKT RX Events: data packets received. In LaserOS mode it climbs fast, as each packet of laser data arrives.
  • DAC Empty Events: how often the buffer ran empty. Lost packets cause this, but so do disabling the interlock or choosing a test pattern.
  • DAC Full Events: how often the buffer overflowed.
  • PKT Loss Events: lost data packets. They’re common over WiFi in congested places, and show as brief drop‑outs in the projection, on and off. Moving away from the congestion helps; a wired connection is better.
  • MAC Address: unique to each cube.
  • Select any of the four counters (PKT RX, DAC Empty, DAC Full, PKT Loss) to reset it to zero.
  • The manual’s pictures also show Temperature, Cube IP, the name (Streaming Host) and address (Streaming IP) of the device streaming to it, and the number of Clients.

Check yourself

Question 1During a busy show, the battery reading on Status Info drops a little, then rises when you stop. Is the battery failing?

The reading is worked out from the battery’s voltage, so it moves around and reads a little lower while the cube projects.

Question 2Streaming over WiFi, the projection drops out briefly every so often. Which line tells you data is being lost?

PKT Loss Events counts lost packets, which show as brief drop‑outs. PKT RX counts packets received.

Question 3The cube is on its charger. What does the manual say about the battery reading?

While the cube charges, its battery charge can’t be measured.

In the manual: Status Info page

Lesson 6 of 8

Not found, or breaking up

This lesson puts the first five together for two problems: LaserOS doesn’t find the cube, or the projection keeps breaking up.

LaserOS doesn’t find the cube

LaserOS finds connected cubes by itself. If its Laser ON button stays greyed out, it hasn’t found one. Check:

  • The control mode: to stream from LaserOS, choose Control Mode → LaserOS.
  • The connection: the status bar shows the connection mode you chose, with a tick, and an IP address (lesson 4). On firmware older than 2.1, the cube must restart after a mode change.
  • The same network: LaserOS finds cubes on the network your phone or computer is on. In WiFi client or LAN client mode with a router, both must be on that router’s network.
  • WiFi modes: the WiFi antenna is connected. In WiFi server mode, your phone or computer has joined the cube’s network (LaserCube, unless you renamed it).
  • A direct cable, LAN client: follow lesson 3’s steps: the computer’s WiFi off and LaserOS closed, the cube’s 169.254 address showing before you start LaserOS, and the computer’s network adapter on automatic.
  • A direct cable, LAN server: the cube gives the computer its address, so leave the computer’s network adapter on automatic too.
  • Older WiFi cubes on the same network: backward compatibility is on, with LaserOS 0.17.7 or later (lesson 4).
  • LaserOS itself: the manual’s set‑up is for LaserOS 0.18.1 or later, from laseros.com.
  • The order: connect your phone or computer to the cube first, then open LaserOS. If LaserOS was already open, quit it completely and open it again.

Five common mistakes, part 1: start order, busy WiFi, LaserOS opened too earlyWatch on YouTube

Plays mistakes 1 to 3. Mistake 1 shows the safety jumper; a US cube starts with the E‑Stop instead (Start here, lesson 6).

The projection keeps breaking up

  • In LaserOS mode, the laser data streams over the network at a high rate, so lost data packets show as brief drop‑outs.
  • Look at Status Info: reset PKT Loss Events to zero, then see whether it climbs while you stream (lesson 5).
  • In WiFi server mode, run WiFi Scan (lesson 2). If most channels are red, the area is congested.
  • Move away from the congestion, or better, use a cable: the USB‑to‑LAN adapter to one device, or LAN client to a router or switch for several cubes (lesson 3).
  • DAC Empty Events also climb when the interlock is disabled or a test pattern is chosen, not only when data is lost.
  • Breaking up in Playlist, Visualizer or Laser Show mode shouldn’t be a WiFi problem: those play from the SD card. The manual warns that cheaper cards can fail to stream the laser shows, and names the Lexar A2 Silver Plus 64GB as the card it tested (Control modes, lesson 2).

Check yourself

Question 1LaserOS is open on your laptop, but the Laser ON button stays greyed out. What does that mean?

A greyed‑out Laser ON button means LaserOS hasn’t found a cube. Check the control mode and the connection.

Question 2A playlist plays from the SD card in Playlist mode, and the neighbours’ WiFi is very busy. Will that make the projection break up?

Playlist mode draws its content from the SD card, so WiFi congestion shouldn’t affect the projection.

Question 3Direct cable, LAN client. You started LaserOS before the cube had its 169.254 address, and LaserOS hasn’t found it. What do the manual’s steps say?

In the manual’s steps, LaserOS isn’t running while the cube gives itself an address, and starts once the address is there.

Lesson 7 of 8

Web admin page, updates and factory reset

In LaserOS mode the cube has a web admin page for some network settings and for firmware updates. There are three ways to update the firmware. A factory reset puts the cube back to its factory firmware and settings; the manual says to do it only if it’s really necessary.

Firmware 2.0: what’s new and how to upgradeWatch on YouTube

The web admin page (LaserOS mode)

  • In LaserOS control mode, the cube has a web admin page (the manual calls it legacy) for some of its network settings and for firmware updates. In Playlist and Visualizer mode, the cube’s address opens that mode’s web app instead.
  • Note the IP address at the bottom right of the cube’s screen. On a phone or computer connected to the cube, type that address into a web browser.
  • Log in with the user name LaserCubeUser and the password Laser2020.
  • Home: the model name, the interlock type, the firmware version and the active firmware partition. The cube has two partitions, so it can fall back to a working state if an update fails or is interrupted.
  • Config → WiFi: the WiFi channel, WiFi server mode’s address, subnet, network name and password, and your router’s network name and password for WiFi client mode. Config → LAN: the address and subnet for LAN server mode. Config → General: the network mode.
  • Update: firmware updates by hand (below).

Three ways to update

The boot screen shows the firmware version when the cube starts (firmware 2.0 and later).

  • From the SD card, the manual’s preferred way: when the card has newer firmware, the cube asks whether to update. The latest card contents, with the newest firmware, are on GitHub. Switch the cube off before you take the card out. Unzip the download and copy its “Lasercube” folder to the root of the card. If the card already has a Lasercube folder, rename the old one first: deleting it also deletes your own playlists and MIDI mappings. Then, with the cube still off, put the card back in and switch it on (Control modes, lesson 2).
  • From LaserOS: when it finds newer firmware, it asks whether to update.
  • By hand, through the web admin page: download the newest update file from the Lasercube/Firmware folder on the same GitHub page. Put the cube in LaserOS control mode and WiFi server mode, and join its network. In a browser, open 192.168.4.1 (the address the manual gives) and log in. On the Update page, click Choose File, pick the update file and click Upload. Wait until the update finishes and the cube restarts.

Factory reset

  • Switch the cube off.
  • Press and hold the knob below the screen (1).
  • Still holding it, switch the cube on with the power switch (2).
  • Keep holding the knob for at least 8 seconds, then let go.
  • The cube goes back to its factory firmware version, and every setting goes back to its default.
  • If the SD card has firmware newer than the factory version, the cube offers to update straight away. Say yes, so it runs the latest firmware.
After a reset

Every setting is back to its default: your connection and network settings, and also the cube’s own projection settings (Settings → Projection, used in the cube’s own modes), including its safety zone. Before the next show, set them again and check the beam block, as in Start here, lesson 8. Power‑On Play is back on too (its default): in Playlist mode, the last playlist starts as soon as the interlock is enabled (Start here, lesson 6).

Check yourself

Put the factory reset in order.

  1. Switch the cube off
  2. Press and hold the knob
  3. Still holding it, switch the cube on
  4. Keep holding for at least 8 seconds, then let go
  5. Accept the update if the SD card offers newer firmware

Hold the knob while you switch on, keep holding for 8 seconds, and take the newer firmware from the SD card if the cube offers it.

Question 1Which way of updating does the manual prefer?

The SD card is the preferred way: when it has newer firmware, the cube asks whether to update.

Question 2You copy the latest card contents to an SD card that already has a Lasercube folder, and you want to keep your own playlists. What do you do with the old folder?

Rename the old folder: deleting it, or formatting the card, also deletes your own playlists and MIDI mappings.

Question 3After a factory reset, what’s back to its default?

The cube goes back to its factory firmware version, and all settings go back to their defaults, the safety zone included.

Question 4You type the cube’s address into a browser and want the web admin page. Which control mode must the cube be in?

The web admin page is there in LaserOS control mode. In Playlist and Visualizer mode, the address opens that mode’s web app.

Lesson 8 of 8

Power, heat and getting help

The right charger, the right temperature, and knowing when to stop using the cube and contact us.

Charger and battery

  • Every LaserCube comes with its 19V DC charger, with a plug or cable for your country. The charger plug is 2.5 × 5.5 mm. The Ultra MK2 and Pro 6W use a 120W charger, the Pro 3W a 65W charger. A 19V charger with a higher wattage is fine.
  • Need a new charger? We recommend a generic 19V DC charger with that plug and at least that wattage, bought locally: shipping one from us usually costs more than the charger.
  • The battery charges while the cube plays on its charger. On battery, the Ultra MK2 runs for 3⁠–⁠4 hours and the Pro for 2⁠–⁠3 hours. Charging takes about 4 hours for the Ultra MK2 and 3 for the Pro.
  • If the battery needs replacing, we recommend a replacement under RMA (a repair return that support arranges) at our service centre: email support@laseros.com with your order number. Don’t open the cube to replace it yourself: the manual doesn’t allow servicing by the user, or opening or modifying the cube.

Heat

  • Use the cube only between 10°C (50°F) and 40°C (104°F). Outside that range, the output can be weak, too strong or swing a lot, and extreme cold or heat will damage the laser diodes. The cube monitors its temperature, so we can tell if a failure was caused by temperature, and such failures aren’t covered by the warranty.
  • Leave open space around all sides, so air can flow through the heat sink underneath.
  • With the improved cooling of the Ultra MK2 and Pro, continuous use should be fine. Switching it on and off often shortens the diodes’ life.
  • Temperature cut‑off: if the cube gets too hot, for example in strong sun, its temperature cut‑off stops it. It doesn’t restart by itself: you have to restart it.

Stop, and contact us

  • Any smell or smoke: a LaserCube should never give off a smell. Unplug it right away, keep it away from anything flammable, don’t charge or use it again, and email support@laseros.com.
  • A suspected defect of any kind, from manufacturing, damage or wear, or a broken manufacturer’s seal: don’t use the cube; contact us.
  • Still not working after the checks in this course: email support@laseros.com. Don’t try to service, modify or fix the laser yourself. If it needs a repair, support tells you how to send it in.

Question 1In strong sun, the cube runs on its charger, stops in the middle of a show and doesn’t come back on. What’s going on?

When it gets too hot, the temperature cut‑off stops the cube, and it doesn’t restart by itself: you restart it. Use it only between 10°C and 40°C.

    I‑LOCK warns that the safety interlock is open, so the laser won’t emit.

    The status bar turns green. What does it mean?

    Green means laser data is arriving over the network. Cyan is the ILDA input.

    What does A‑IP before the address in the status bar mean?

    A‑IP marks an APIPA address that the cube gave itself, starting 169.254.

    Which line on Status Info counts lost data packets?

    PKT Loss Events counts lost packets; they show as brief drop‑outs.

    Besides lost packets, what can make DAC Empty Events climb?

    The buffer also runs empty when the interlock is disabled or a test pattern is chosen.

    How do you set a counter like PKT Loss Events back to zero?

    Selecting the counter on the Status Info page resets it to zero.

    LaserOS is open, but its Laser ON button stays greyed out. What does it mean?

    A greyed‑out Laser ON button means LaserOS hasn’t found a cube: check the control mode and the connection.

    Streaming over WiFi, the projection breaks up and PKT Loss Events keeps climbing. What helps most, says the manual?

    Lost packets come from congested WiFi. Moving away helps; a wired connection is better.

    A built‑in laser show breaks up, played from the SD card. What does the manual warn about?

    Laser Show mode plays from the card, and the manual warns that cheaper cards can fail to stream the shows.

    Which way of updating the firmware does the manual prefer?

    The SD card: when it has newer firmware, the cube asks whether to update.

    Besides the SD card, what can also offer you a firmware update?

    When LaserOS finds newer firmware, it asks whether to update. The SD card is the manual’s preferred way.

    Updating the SD card, you delete the old Lasercube folder before copying the new one. What do you lose?

    Deleting the old folder deletes your own playlists and MIDI mappings with it. Rename it instead to keep them; the new folder brings the built‑in shows.

    What do you log in to the web admin page with?

    The user name is LaserCubeUser and the password Laser2020. LaserCube is the name of the cube’s own WiFi network.

    On the web admin page, where do you set the address for LAN server mode?

    Config → LAN sets the address and subnet for LAN server mode; Config → WiFi is for the WiFi modes.

    An update is interrupted. What lets the cube get back to a working state?

    The cube has two firmware partitions, so it can fall back if an update fails or is interrupted.

    During a factory reset, how long do you keep holding the knob after switching on?

    Hold the knob while you switch on, keep holding for at least 8 seconds, then let go.

    After a factory reset, what about the safety zone set on the cube itself (Settings → Projection)?

    Every setting goes back to its default, the safety zone included. Set it again and check the beam block before the next show.

    After a reset, the cube offers newer firmware from the SD card. What does the manual say?

    The manual says to update, so the cube runs the latest firmware.

    You’ve lost the charger of your Pro 6W. Which replacement fits what we recommend?

    The Pro 6W comes with a 19V DC, 120W charger with a 2.5 × 5.5 mm plug. We recommend a replacement with that plug and at least that wattage; a higher wattage is fine.

    The cube gives off a burning smell, but it still works. What do you do?

    Any smell or smoke: unplug it right away, keep it away from anything flammable, don’t charge or use it again, and email support@laseros.com.

    The temperature cut‑off has stopped the cube. What happens next?

    After the temperature cut‑off, the cube doesn’t restart by itself: you restart it.

    The battery needs replacing. What do we recommend?

    We recommend a battery replacement under RMA at our service centre: email support@laseros.com with your order number. The manual doesn’t allow opening or modifying the cube.

    Between which temperatures may you use the cube?

    The manual’s range is 10°C (50°F) to 40°C (104°F). Extreme cold or heat will damage the laser diodes.

    What does the cube need around it while it runs?

    Leave open space around all sides, so air can flow through the heat sink underneath.
